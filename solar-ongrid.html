How It Works

The grid becomes your battery

An on-grid system connects your solar array directly to your building's supply through a grid-tie inverter. Whatever your loads need is taken from solar first; anything left over flows out through a bi-directional meter and is credited to your account.

At night or on dull days, you simply draw from the grid as before. At billing time the discom nets the two directions against each other, which is why the arrangement is called net metering. Because there is no battery bank, the cost and the maintenance burden both drop sharply.

  • No batteries to buy, replace or maintain
  • Highest efficiency — no conversion losses into and out of storage
  • Surplus generation credited rather than wasted
  • Compact equipment footprint, no dedicated battery room
  • Typically the fastest payback of the three system types

System Components

What Goes Into an On-Grid Plant

Six things determine whether a grid-tied system performs for twenty-five years or becomes a service headache.

Solar Modules

Tier-1 mono PERC modules with standard product warranty and a long-term performance warranty on output.

Grid-Tie Inverter

Converts DC to grid-synchronised AC, with MPPT tracking and mandatory anti-islanding protection.

Bi-Directional Meter

Installed by the discom after approval. Records import and export separately for net billing.

DCDB & ACDB

Protection boxes with fuses, isolators and surge protection on both the DC and AC sides.

Earthing & Lightning Arrestor

Separate earth pits and a lightning arrestor covering the array — non-negotiable on any roof.

Mounting Structure

Hot-dip galvanised structure at the right tilt, engineered for local wind loading.

Application

Registration with the discom along with load details and system specifications.

2

Technical Feasibility

3

Installation

The plant is built and tested while the application is in process.

4

Inspection

Discom or electrical inspector visits and verifies the installation.

5

Meter Change

The bi-directional meter is installed and net billing begins.

Is It Right For You?

On-Grid: Strengths and Limits

An honest look at where grid-tied systems win and where they do not.

ConsiderationOn-Grid Position
Backup during outageNone — the inverter shuts down for lineman safety
Upfront costLowest of the three system types
MaintenancePanel cleaning and an annual check; no battery upkeep
Surplus generationExported and credited through net metering
Approval neededYes — discom sanction is mandatory before commissioning
Ideal forHomes and businesses with reasonably reliable grid supply
If power cuts are frequent at your location, a hybrid system is usually the better answer. It keeps the bill savings of on-grid while adding battery backup for essential loads. See our Off-Grid & Hybrid page.
